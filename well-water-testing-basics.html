Well Water Testing Basics

Testing is the foundation for every other decision you make about a well. Here's a practical way to think about it.

When to test

  • When you move into a home with a well.
  • On a regular schedule. Your state or local health department can tell you what it recommends.
  • After flooding, well repairs, or a change in taste, smell or color.
  • When someone in the home is pregnant or there's an infant.

Where to test

A state-certified laboratory gives you results you can rely on. Your health department can usually point you to certified labs and may tell you which tests are common for your area. Water System Guide's well water testing guide goes into what a typical panel covers.

Questions to ask the lab

  • Which tests do you recommend for wells in this area, and why?
  • What are the sampling instructions and how quickly must the sample arrive?
  • Will the report show the result next to a reference limit?

Collecting the sample

Follow the lab's instructions exactly. Bacteria samples in particular are easy to contaminate. Use the bottle provided, don't rinse it unless told to, and keep track of the time limit.

If the results raise questions, the companion site Reading Your Water Report explains common terms in plain language.
