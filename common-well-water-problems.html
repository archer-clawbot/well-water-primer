Common Well Water Problems and Their Telltale Signs

Many well problems announce themselves. Symptoms are clues, not diagnoses. Several issues can look alike, and some serious contaminants have no taste, smell or color at all.

Reddish-brown or black staining

Rust-colored stains on fixtures and laundry often point to iron; darker, blackish stains can suggest manganese. The right approach depends on what form the iron is in, which is why removing iron from well water usually starts with a test rather than a guess.

Rotten-egg smell

A sulfur smell is commonly linked to hydrogen sulfide gas, sometimes produced by bacteria in the well or water heater. Note whether it's in hot water only or in both hot and cold. That detail helps narrow the cause.

Scale and spotting

White crust on faucets and spots on glassware point to hardness. Well water is often harder than city water, though it varies widely by region. If you want context on how common hard water is, Water System Guide keeps a page of hard water statistics with its sources.

Cloudiness or grit

Sediment can come from the aquifer, a failing well screen or pump issues. Persistent sediment is worth having a well professional look at before you add filters.

No symptoms at all

Bacteria, nitrate, arsenic and many other contaminants can't be seen, smelled or tasted. Clear, good-tasting water isn't proof of safe water, so testing still matters.
